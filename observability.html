Zen Analytics Workbench

Point it at your running system

A model built from configuration is a prediction. Useful before anything exists — and still a guess. The workbench is also an OpenTelemetry receiver: point any exporter at it and the guesses are replaced, node by node, with what your system is actually doing.

01

No collector, no export step

The app is the receiver. POST /v1/traces and /v1/metrics, OTLP/HTTP, protobuf or JSON, gzipped or not. Protobuf is decoded by a small dependency-free wire reader, so the default exporter configuration just works — nothing to switch, nothing to install between you and it.

OTEL_SERVICE_NAME=orders-api \
OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_ENDPOINT=http://127.0.0.1:4173 \
OTEL_TRACES_EXPORTER=otlp \
  node --require @opentelemetry/auto-instrumentations-node/register app.js

Telemetry is held in a bounded in-memory window — 20,000 spans / 300 seconds by default, both configurable. Nothing is written to disk, nothing survives a restart, and nothing leaves your machine.

02

The join everything depends on

Your deployment calls it orders-api. Your design calls it Order Service. Exact matching means nothing lands — and a canvas that silently stayed modelled is worse than one that admits it is unbound.

So binding is explicit and visible. Every observed service is listed with what it is doing and where it belongs, with fuzzy suggestions you accept in one click. Until a service is bound, its numbers are shown but never applied.

04

Demand is not capacity

One detail decides whether the whole thing tells the truth. Observed traffic is recorded as offered load — never as the throughput ceiling.

The ceiling is what saturation divides by. Write measured traffic into it and every observed node appears saturated by its own load, at precisely the moment you started trusting the tool.

05

Provenance, not flattery

Two questions people habitually conflate, kept apart on purpose:

Provenance — what the badge says

How much of this workspace is backed by observed telemetry rather than catalog estimates. A workspace crosses to telemetry-validated at 50% coverage. It is a fact about where the numbers came from.

Agreement — the second number

How closely the generic model predicted what you measured. A quality signal about the model. A measured node whose model disagrees is still a measured node.

Blurring them would let a badge reading validated quietly mean “the model guessed well”, or bury real telemetry behind a model that needs fitting. The calibration report keeps the second honest: observed versus modelled p50/p99 per node, with the residual and whether it is in band.

06

Self time: waiting, or working

Every span carries its parent, so a trace is a call tree — a distributed one, crossing services in a way a single-process stack cannot. Subtract the time a span's children were running and you get its self time.

What it means

“What is slow” and “where did the time go” are different questions. Flattening spans into per-symbol aggregates only ever answers the first.

What to do

Check self time before reaching for a profiler. If the span is waiting, no amount of CPU profiling will find anything.

07

And the agent gets it too

The zen-ground skill and the zen-sidecar MCP server hand this to a coding agent: wire the exporter, build a test harness where none exists, verify context propagation — before anyone reaches for an APM vendor. The agent's picture of the codebase is then calibrated by real scale data, not static structure alone.

Privacy

Memory-only, local, never written to disk and never forwarded. The receiver takes what a process on your machine posts to it, holds it for five minutes, and forgets it.
